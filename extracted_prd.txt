1. Executive Summary
BharatMile is an India-focused travel and tourism platform designed to help domestic and
international travellers discover destinations, plan itineraries, and book affordable travel experiences
across the length and breadth of India. This document defines the product vision, target users,
feature set, success metrics, and release roadmap for the v1.0 public launch.
India's domestic travel market is one of the fastest-growing in the world, projected to surpass USD
50 billion by 2028. BharatMile aims to capture a meaningful share of this market by focusing on
budget-conscious travellers, experience-based tourism, and underserved Tier-2 and Tier-3
destinations that larger OTAs overlook.

2. Problem Statement

Key user pain-points the product addresses:
Fragmented discovery

Travellers must browse multiple websites to research destinations, hotels,
transport, and activities.

Metro-centric content

Most travel platforms over-index on popular metros and miss thousands of
remarkable smaller destinations.

Budget opacity

Hidden costs and unclear pricing make it difficult to plan a trip on a fixed
budget.

Language & localisation
gap

English-only interfaces alienate a large segment of regional-language
speakers.

Lack of personalisation

Generic itinerary suggestions do not account for travel style, group
composition, or past preferences.

3. Goals & Non-Goals

Goals
• Create a single-stop discovery and planning platform for Indian travel.
• Surface authentic, budget-friendly experiences across all 36 states and union territories.
• Provide transparent pricing with no hidden fees at the point of discovery.
• Support at least 6 Indian regional languages at launch.
• Build a community layer (reviews, photos, trip stories) that generates organic content.
• Achieve a seamless mobile-first experience (PWA + native apps).

Non-Goals (v1.0)

• International travel booking or visa assistance.
• White-label B2B platform for travel agents.
• In-house hotel or transport operations — we are a marketplace, not an operator.
• Real-time flight booking (planned for v2.0).

4. Target Users & Personas

■ The Budget
Backpacker

Age 20–30, college students and young professionals seeking affordable
weekend getaways and offbeat destinations within a ■5,000–■15,000 budget
per trip.

■■■■■■■ The Family
Planner

Age 30–45, planning annual family holidays. Values convenience, safety,
kid-friendly activities, and clear all-inclusive pricing.

■ The Experience
Seeker

Age 25–40, interested in wellness retreats, cultural immersion, adventure
sports, and culinary tourism rather than standard sightseeing.

■ The NRI Traveller

Indian diaspora travelling back to India 1–2 times per year who need
up-to-date, reliable destination information in English.

■■ The Solo Explorer

Age 22–35, solo travellers (especially solo women) who need safety
information, community reviews, and flexible single-traveller packages.

7. Technical Architecture Overview
Frontend

Next.js 14 (App Router) — SSR for SEO, React for interactivity. Tailwind
CSS. PWA support.

Mobile Apps

React Native (iOS & Android) — v2.0 roadmap.

Backend API

Node.js / TypeScript with NestJS. RESTful + GraphQL endpoints.

Database

PostgreSQL (relational data), Redis (caching & sessions), Elasticsearch
(search).

AI / ML

LLM-powered trip planner via API (Claude / GPT-4). Personalisation via
collaborative filtering.

Content CDN

Cloudflare CDN for images and static assets. WebP/AVIF format with lazy
loading.

Payments

Razorpay for UPI, cards, EMI. PCI-DSS compliant.

Analytics

Mixpanel (product analytics), Google Analytics 4, Hotjar (session
recordings).

Infrastructure

AWS (ap-south-1, Mumbai). ECS + Fargate. RDS Aurora. S3 +
CloudFront.

Monitoring

Datadog APM, PagerDuty on-call, Sentry for error tracking.

8. Non-Functional Requirements
Performance

Core Web Vitals green. LCP < 2.5s, FID < 100ms, CLS < 0.1 on 4G
networks.

Availability

99.9% uptime SLA. Zero-downtime deployments.

Scalability

Handle 100k concurrent users at launch; horizontally scalable to 1M+.

Security

OWASP Top 10 mitigated. Data encrypted at rest (AES-256) and in transit
(TLS 1.3). VAPT before launch.

Accessibility

WCAG 2.1 AA compliance across all pages.

SEO

Server-side rendering for all content pages. Structured data
(Schema.org/TouristAttraction). Sitemap auto-generation.

Localisation

i18n framework; RTL support for Urdu; 6 languages at launch: Hindi, Tamil,
Telugu, Kannada, Bengali, Marathi.

Data Privacy

IT Act 2000 + DPDP Act 2023 compliant. Explicit consent for data
collection. Right to erasure.

11. Risks & Mitigations
Content quality at scale

HIGH

Hire regional content specialists; UGC moderation pipeline with
AI + human review.

Low initial organic traffic

HIGH

6-month SEO content sprint pre-launch; destination guides
targeting long-tail keywords.

Payment gateway failures

MEDIUM

Multi-PSP fallback (Razorpay + PayU); retry logic; real-time
monitoring.

OTA competition
(MakeMyTrip, EaseMyTrip)

HIGH

Differentiate on depth (Tier-2/3 destinations), UGC, and budget
transparency.

Regulatory (DPDP Act
2023)

MEDIUM

Legal counsel engaged; consent management platform
integrated at launch.

Seasonal traffic spikes

MEDIUM

Auto-scaling infra; load tested at 5× peak before launch.

12. Open Questions
• Should BharatMile operate its own hotel/package inventory (merchant model) or remain purely
a marketplace? Decision required by Sprint 3.
• Which 6 regional languages are prioritised for v1.0? Recommendation: Hindi, Tamil, Telugu,
Kannada, Bengali, Marathi — awaiting stakeholder sign-off.
• Monetisation split between affiliate commission, listing fees, and premium subscriptions —
finalise revenue model before Beta.
• Should the AI Trip Planner be powered in-house or via API? Evaluate build vs. buy in Sprint 3.
• Partnership structure with IRCTC and State Tourism Boards — legal/BD to initiate
conversations by Sprint 2.
